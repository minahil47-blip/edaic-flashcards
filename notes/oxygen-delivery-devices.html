Oxygen delivery devices

Fixed versus variable performance, the Venturi colours, and why the numbers on a nasal cannula are only ever approximate.

The governing principle

  • Peak inspiratory flow in a breathless adult is 30–60 L/min — far higher than any low-flow device delivers.
  • A device that delivers less than peak inspiratory flow is variable performance: the patient entrains room air to make up the difference, so the delivered FiO₂ falls as their respiratory effort rises.
  • A device that delivers more than peak inspiratory flow is fixed performance: the FiO₂ is what the device says, regardless of the patient's breathing.

  • Never run a simple face mask below 6 L/min. The mask body is a reservoir of exhaled gas — below 6 L/min the flow is insufficient to flush it, and the patient rebreathes CO₂.

Non-rebreathing (reservoir) mask

  • Flow 10–15 L/min, delivering roughly 80–100% in practice.
  • A reservoir bag plus one-way flaps that prevent entrainment of room air on inspiration and prevent exhaled gas entering the bag.
  • Realistic delivered FiO₂ depends on the flaps: both flaps removed 80–85% · one flap removed 85–90% · both flaps in place 95–100%.
  • Ensure the reservoir bag stays inflated throughout inspiration — if it collapses, the flow is too low.

Venturi masks — fixed performance

Air is entrained in a fixed ratio through a jet, so total flow exceeds peak inspiratory flow and the FiO₂ is genuinely fixed.

Two points that get examined

  • The entrainment ratio falls as FiO₂ rises — the 24% valve entrains about 25 volumes of air per volume of oxygen, the 60% valve only about 1. So at 60% the total flow may no longer exceed peak inspiratory flow, and the "fixed performance" claim weakens at the top of the range.
  • Use a Venturi in the patient at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure (target 88–92%), where a predictable FiO₂ matters more than a high one.
