Sepsis, liver failure and trauma

Sepsis-3 and the hour-one bundle, the anaesthetic consequences of liver failure, damage control resuscitation, and how renal and hepatic disease change the preoperative plan.

Sepsis

Sepsis-3 definitions (2016) abandoned SIRS criteria. Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection — operationalised as an acute rise in SOFA ≥ 2.

TermDefinitionMortality
SepsisInfection + acute SOFA rise ≥ 2≈ 10%
Septic shockSepsis + vasopressor to keep MAP ≥ 65 AND lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid≈ 40%

The hour-one bundle

  • Measure lactate and remeasure if > 2 mmol/L.
  • Blood cultures before antibiotics — but never delay antibiotics to obtain them.
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics: every hour of delay in septic shock increases mortality.
  • 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥ 4, then reassess rather than continuing blindly.
  • Vasopressors to MAP ≥ 65 mmHg if hypotensive during or after fluid.

The points that catch people out

  • Noradrenaline is first-line, not dopamine — dopamine causes more arrhythmia with no benefit. Vasopressin is added to spare noradrenaline dose; adrenaline is third-line.
  • Balanced crystalloid is preferred to 0.9% saline; starches are contraindicated (renal failure, mortality); albumin is an option after large crystalloid volumes.
  • Early goal-directed therapy is dead. ProCESS, ARISE and ProMISe found no benefit over good usual care — central venous oxygen saturation targets and mandated CVP targets are gone.
  • Hydrocortisone for shock requiring ongoing vasopressors; it shortens time to shock reversal, with inconsistent mortality effect.
  • Transfuse at Hb < 70 g/L (TRISS) unless bleeding or ischaemia. Restrictive beats liberal.
  • Lactate in sepsis is not purely anaerobic — adrenergic stimulation of glycolysis and reduced clearance contribute. It is a marker of severity and of response, not a direct oxygen-debt reading.
  • Source control — drainage, debridement, device removal — is as important as any drug, and is the commonest reason a patient fails to improve.

Drugs in liver failure

  • Atracurium and cisatracurium are the relaxants of choice — Hofmann elimination is organ-independent.
  • Remifentanil is the opioid of choice — plasma esterase hydrolysis, no accumulation. Morphine's active metabolites accumulate; avoid.
  • Propofol is acceptable: high clearance and short context-sensitive half-time, though recovery is a little prolonged.
  • Paracetamol is not absolutely contraindicated in chronic liver disease — it remains the safest simple analgesic, at a reduced dose. NSAIDs are what must be avoided (renal failure, variceal bleeding).
  • Avoid halothane (hepatitis), suxamethonium with caution (reduced cholinesterase prolongs block).

Scores and specifics

  • Child–Pugh: bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy. MELD: bilirubin, INR, creatinine (± sodium) — used for transplant allocation and for perioperative risk.
  • Paracetamol overdose: N-acetylcysteine, and the King's College criteria for transplant referral — pH < 7.3 after resuscitation, or the triad of INR > 6.5, creatinine > 300 µmol/L and grade III–IV encephalopathy.
  • Variceal bleeding: terlipressin, antibiotics, endoscopic banding, balloon tamponade as a bridge, TIPS for failure. Antibiotic prophylaxis improves survival.
  • Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis: ascitic neutrophils > 250/mm³. Treat, then prophylax.
  • Transfusion in liver disease is guided by viscoelastic testing and clinical bleeding, not by correcting the INR to a number — over-transfusion raises portal pressure and worsens bleeding.

Trauma

  1. C

    Catastrophic haemorrhage first

    <C>ABC — tourniquet, haemostatic dressing, direct pressure, pelvic binder. Exsanguination kills faster than airway obstruction in penetrating and blast injury.

  2. DCR

    Damage control resuscitation

    Permissive hypotension until haemorrhage control (not in head injury), blood products in balanced ratio rather than crystalloid, tranexamic acid within 3 h (CRASH-2), active warming, early calcium.

  3. DCS

    Damage control surgery

    Arrest bleeding and contamination, pack and temporise, correct physiology in ICU, return for definitive repair. Operating for hours on a cold, acidotic, coagulopathic patient is the error it exists to prevent.

  4. △

    The lethal triad

    Hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy, each worsening the others — with hypocalcaemia often added as a fourth. Clotting factor function falls steeply below 34 °C and below pH 7.2.

Trauma-induced coagulopathy

  • It is present on arrival in a quarter of major trauma patients, before any dilution. Driven by tissue injury and shock, with activated protein C, hyperfibrinolysis and platelet dysfunction — not simply dilution of factors.
  • Fibrinogen falls first; replace with cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate, guided by viscoelastic testing where available.
  • Tranexamic acid works only early — benefit within 3 hours, with evidence of harm when given later.
  • Massive transfusion: aim for a ratio approaching 1:1:1 red cells, plasma and platelets; watch ionised calcium, potassium and temperature (see Massive transfusion).

Specific trauma points

  • Head injury — permissive hypotension is contraindicated; a single episode of hypotension or hypoxia worsens outcome. Maintain cerebral perfusion pressure, normocapnia 4.5 to 5.0 kPa, 30° head up, no hypotonic fluid. CPP = MAP − ICP, from mean arterial pressure, never systolic.
  • Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis treated immediately, not a radiological one.
  • Cardiac tamponade — Beck's triad, pulsus paradoxus, electrical alternans; needs drainage, and positive pressure ventilation may precipitate arrest.
  • Spinal cord injury — neurogenic shock (hypotension with bradycardia, distinguishing it from haemorrhage); suxamethonium is safe in the first 24 to 48 h, dangerous thereafter. High-dose steroids are no longer recommended.
  • Burns — Parkland formula, early intubation for airway burns, carboxyhaemoglobin and cyanide, suxamethonium safe only in the first 24 h (see CO poisoning & burns).
  • Fat embolism — long bone fracture, 24 to 72 h later, hypoxaemia, confusion and petechiae; supportive treatment, early fracture fixation prevents it.

AKI staging (KDIGO) — the minimum to remember

  • Stage 1: creatinine 1.5 to 1.9 × baseline, or a rise ≥ 26 µmol/L, or urine < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 to 12 h.
  • Stage 2: 2.0 to 2.9 × baseline, or urine < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12 h.
  • Stage 3: ≥ 3 × baseline, or creatinine ≥ 354 µmol/L, or renal replacement started, or anuria ≥ 12 h.
  • Indications for renal replacement: refractory hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload, uraemic complications (pericarditis, encephalopathy) and some poisonings — not a creatinine number alone.
