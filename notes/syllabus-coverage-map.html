Syllabus coverage map

The 16 UEMS domains that EDAIC questions are written against — and an honest audit of what you have.

Where this taxonomy comes from

  • ESAIC states that all EDAIC questions are created to match the domains set by the UEMS European Training Requirements in Anaesthesiology. The ETR is therefore the authoritative topic list — not any commercial question bank's own categories.
  • Paper A — basic sciences: anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, physics and clinical measurement, statistics.
    Paper B — clinical anaesthesia, intensive care, emergency medicine, pain.
  • Format: 60 questions per paper, one stem with five independent true/false statements, +1 per correct statement and no negative marking. Each paper is scored out of 300. Standard is criterion-referenced — there is no published fixed pass percentage.
  • The ETR note that basic sciences are "understood as a prerequisite" rather than listed separately is a trap: Paper A tests them heavily. They are listed in full below regardless.

The priority list

Highest yield gaps — do these first

  • 1. Difficult airway algorithms — a named ETR item, guaranteed to appear, and currently cards only.
  • 2. ARDS & mechanical ventilation — modes, lung-protective ventilation, weaning. Nothing at all.
  • 3. Sepsis & the sepsis bundle — named explicitly, nothing at all.
  • 4. Trauma & major haemorrhage — an entire ETR sub-list with no coverage.
  • 5. Statistics & study design — a named Paper A subject sitting on cards alone.
  • 6. Patient safety & human factors (1.8 + 1.10) — two whole domains missing, and they are easy marks because the content is finite and memorable.

Second tier

  • Neuroanaesthesia as its own note (ICP, CPP, neuroprotection, VAE) · LAST and local anaesthetic pharmacology · truncal blocks · PACU and discharge criteria · ICU scoring systems · CPB/ECMO · congenital heart disease · RRT · procedural sedation and remote-site anaesthesia · chronic pain.

What you already have that is genuinely strong

  • Physiology — near-complete coverage, and the diagrams are better than most textbooks.
  • Obstetrics — three notes covering a domain many candidates neglect.
  • Transfusion and coagulation — four notes including TEG traces and blood groups.
  • Endocrine emergencies — DKA/HHS and adrenal are done to viva depth.
