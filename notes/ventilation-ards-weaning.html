Ventilation: ARDS, weaning and the machines

Lung-protective ventilation, the rescue therapies that work and those that do not, weaning and extubation, and how theatre and ICU ventilators actually differ.

ARDS

Berlin definition: onset within 1 week of a known insult, bilateral opacities not explained by effusion or collapse, not primarily cardiogenic (no left atrial hypertension), and hypoxaemia measured on PEEP ≥ 5 cmH₂O.

Lung-protective ventilation — the numbers

  • Tidal volume 6 mL/kg predicted body weight — predicted from height and sex, never actual weight. This is the one intervention with an unambiguous mortality benefit (ARDSNet).
  • Plateau pressure < 30 cmH₂O. Plateau is measured on an inspiratory hold and reflects alveolar pressure; peak pressure includes airway resistance and is the wrong target.
  • Driving pressure = plateau − PEEP, target < 15 cmH₂O. It is the strongest single predictor of mortality in the pooled analyses, better than tidal volume or plateau alone.
  • Permissive hypercapnia is accepted, with pH tolerated to about 7.2 — the cost of protecting the lung.
  • Higher PEEP benefits moderate-to-severe ARDS but not mild; it is titrated, not set by protocol. Recruitment manoeuvres with aggressive PEEP titration increased mortality in the ART trial.

Rescue therapies, ranked by evidence

  • Prone positioning ≥ 16 h/day in severe ARDS (P/F < 13.3 kPa) — PROSEVA showed a clear mortality benefit. It improves V/Q matching, recruits dorsal lung and makes the transpulmonary pressure gradient more uniform. Hazards: lines, tube displacement, pressure areas, facial oedema.
  • Neuromuscular blockade early in severe ARDS — benefit in ACURASYS, not reproduced in ROSE. Reasonable for severe dyssynchrony, not routine.
  • Inhaled nitric oxide improves oxygenation but not mortality, and increases renal impairment. Prostacyclin likewise.
  • ECMO as a bridge in the most severe — CESAR and EOLIA. It supports; it does not treat the lung.
  • Conservative fluid strategy (FACTT) shortens ventilation without harming kidneys. Steroids have a role in some phenotypes and in COVID-19 ARDS; routine use in all ARDS is not established.
  • High-frequency oscillation increased mortality in OSCILLATE and did not help in OSCAR — abandoned.

Ventilator-induced lung injury

  • Volutrauma — overdistension by excess tidal volume. The dominant mechanism, and the reason the target is a volume, not a pressure.
  • Barotrauma — high alveolar pressure causing air leak: pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, surgical emphysema.
  • Atelectrauma — repeated opening and collapse of unstable alveoli. The argument for adequate PEEP.
  • Biotrauma — the inflammatory cascade released into the circulation, linking the ventilator to distant organ failure.
  • Oxygen toxicity — absorption atelectasis and free-radical injury with prolonged high FiO₂. Target the lowest FiO₂ that achieves an acceptable saturation; hyperoxia is not benign.
  • Patient self-inflicted lung injury — vigorous spontaneous effort generating large transpulmonary swings even without a ventilator, which is part of the argument for early intubation in some patients.

Weaning and extubation

  1. 1

    Daily screening

    Resolving cause, FiO₂ ≤ 0.4 with PEEP ≤ 5 to 8, haemodynamically stable with minimal vasopressors, awake and cooperative, adequate cough. Screen every day — delayed weaning is as harmful as premature extubation.

  2. 2

    Spontaneous breathing trial

    30 minutes to 2 hours on T-piece, CPAP or low-level pressure support. Success predicts extubation better than any single index.

  3. 3

    The indices

    Rapid shallow breathing index = respiratory rate ÷ tidal volume in litres; < 105 predicts success. Negative inspiratory force more negative than −20 to −25 cmH₂O; vital capacity > 10 to 15 mL/kg. All are adjuncts to the trial, not substitutes.

  4. 4

    Extubate, then support

    Cuff leak test where laryngeal oedema is a concern. Prophylactic non-invasive ventilation or high-flow nasal oxygen reduces reintubation in high-risk patients — but NIV used to rescue established post-extubation failure delays reintubation and worsens outcome.

Why weaning fails

  • Load exceeds capacity. Load: bronchospasm, secretions, oedema, abdominal distension, auto-PEEP. Capacity: diaphragm weakness, critical illness myopathy, sedation, electrolyte depletion (phosphate, magnesium, potassium).
  • Weaning-induced cardiac failure — the negative intrathoracic pressure of spontaneous breathing increases venous return and LV afterload, unmasking left ventricular dysfunction. A characteristically overlooked cause of repeated SBT failure.
  • Daily sedation interruption and early mobilisation shorten ventilation; this is the ABCDEF bundle territory, with light sedation targets (see RASS & CAM-ICU).
  • Tracheostomy reduces sedation need and improves comfort, but early tracheostomy has not shown a mortality benefit (TracMan).

Intrinsic PEEP and the capnogram

  • Auto-PEEP (gas trapping) from inadequate expiratory time — asthma, COPD, high rate. It causes hypotension, raised plateau pressure, trigger failure and barotrauma. Treat by lengthening expiration: lower rate, shorter inspiratory time, treat bronchospasm, and disconnect to allow full exhalation if circulation is compromised.
  • Measure it with an expiratory hold; measure plateau with an inspiratory hold. Two different manoeuvres, frequently swapped in questions.
  • Compliance = ΔV / ΔP. Static compliance uses plateau pressure; dynamic uses peak and so includes resistance. A rising peak with an unchanged plateau means a resistance problem (secretions, kinked tube, bronchospasm); both rising means a compliance problem (oedema, pneumothorax, abdominal distension).

Workstation safety points

  • Soda lime ≈ 94% calcium hydroxide with sodium and potassium hydroxide; it absorbs CO₂ exothermically, producing water and heat, and an indicator dye signals exhaustion. Granule size 4 to 8 mesh balances surface area against resistance and channelling.
  • Sevoflurane with dry soda lime produces compound A; desflurane and isoflurane with dry, especially baralyme, absorbent produce carbon monoxide. Both are reasons not to leave the machine running dry over a weekend.
  • Check the machine before every list: power, gas supplies and cylinder reserves, a leak test of the breathing system, the vaporiser filled and seated, the correct one-way valve function, suction, and the self-inflating bag as the ultimate backup.
  • A circle system's inspired concentration lags the dial markedly at low fresh gas flow, which is why agent monitoring, not the dial, tells you what the patient is receiving.
